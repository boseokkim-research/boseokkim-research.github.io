Research programme

Children and adolescents developing in context.

How do culture, relationships and educational experiences shape young people’s autonomy and wellbeing?

My research focuses on school-age children and adolescents, connecting lived experience with developmental patterns and the design of responsive educational interventions.

01 / Culture & relational wellbeing

Qualitative inquiry into lived experience

How does culture shape wellbeing?

My doctoral research examines the lived experience of gratitude among South Korean adolescents. This programme asks how cultural and relational contexts shape gratitude, and what these experiences mean for education. The STARR framework is part of this developing work.

Kim & McLellan (2026) · Cambridge Journal of Education ↗

02 / Autonomy & wellbeing across childhood and adolescence

Longitudinal analysis · autoregressive latent trajectory modelling

How do relationships support young people’s autonomy and wellbeing?

A six-wave South Korean panel study followed a baseline sample of 2,590 adolescents from approximately ages 13 to 18. Perceived parental autonomy support declined on average across the modelled period. It was prospectively associated with life satisfaction from age 16 to 17 and from age 17 to 18.

Kim & Jeong (2026) · Journal of Adolescence ↗

03 / Responsive educational interventions

Quasi-experimental field research · moderation analysis

Why do interventions work differently for different learners?

A quasi-experimental field trial with 549 fifth- and sixth-grade students in South Korea compared signature-strength, growth-strength and combined interventions with an active control. Overall, the intervention conditions did not significantly outperform the control. Among students with lower school adjustment, the growth-strength condition was associated with greater gains in autonomy and competence.

Kim & Shin (2025) · International Journal of Applied Positive Psychology ↗

Developing directions

From understanding to educational practice.

Ongoing work explores gratitude experiences, the educational use of generative AI, and parental autonomy support, control and academic helplessness. These studies extend the programme’s attention to context and individual differences.
